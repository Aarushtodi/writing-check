# Writing rulebook

## The stack (all free, runs on your computer)
- Vale: style rules. Runs MyRules (custom), write-good, proselint and a trimmed Microsoft style.
- Harper: grammar, spelling, repeated words.
- LanguageTool: closest free thing to Grammarly's grammar engine. Needs Java, which the installer brings along.

## The custom rules (styles/MyRules)
- No dashes of any kind, no underscores or ___ blanks, no exclamation points outside friends and family.
- No "it's not X, it's Y" contrast tic.
- AITells: delve, leverage, robust, seamless, "I wanted to reach out".
- AIInflation: "serves as", "pivotal moment", "cutting edge", "empowering", "remarkable".
- ChatbotResidue: "Great question", "hope this message finds you", "let's dive in".
- BorrowedAuthority: "experts believe", "studies show". Suggestion level.
- Apology: padding like "just wanted to". Suggestion level.
- Taken from Grammarly's category list: BlandWords, NumberStart, SmallNumbers, SentenceLength (30 words), SplitInfinitive, QuotePunctuation, SlashSpacing.

## Humanizer
From blader/humanizer (MIT), built on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing". Word level tells are Vale rules. Structure tells (closers, forced threes, same openings, explaining before the ask) are a judgment pass in skill/check/references/humanizer.md.

## What the tools cannot decide
- Register: officials, teachers and coaches get capitals, apostrophes, full sentences. Friends and parents stay casual.
- Drafts: lead with the ask, short, no setup lines.
- Long pieces: keep hedges visible, no punchline endings, plain verbs.
- Tools flag, the writer decides. Passive voice and first person flags are suggestions only.

## Off on purpose
Passive voice, Oxford comma, starting a sentence with a conjunction, fragments, curly quotes.

## Not used
Grammarly itself. Its rules are private and its checking happens on Grammarly's servers, so there is nothing to run locally.
